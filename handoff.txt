## Blocker for step 2: VibeTour can't play video packs yet

The standard says so (status line, and "Engine work this standard assumes"). The tours so far are mp4s that
`build_trip.sh` and `composite.sh` render offline. Playing the tour in the IDE needs some engine work first:
- a Video Director that turns the `motion` and `journey` snapshot into a playlist (cruise clips, stop sets, transitions,
  dissolves, playback rate from speed);
- the cockpit layer in the webview;
- packs loaded from disk (today packs are bundled at build time, in `src/packs/index.ts`), with `localResourceRoots` and a
  static route on the companion server;
- the `media` block in `JourneyPack` and its validator;
- a codec check for H.264 in VS Code webviews.

**Decision A: decided on 16 October, staged, as recommended.** Asked again, the user said "build Hybrid version per your
own recommendation". That recommendation is the "suggested fit" below:
- Milestone 1 plays baked clips, and step 2's IDE test uses it.
- The live compositor is built during step 3, and step 4's retest runs on it.
- Hi-res is then plates at 1080p and the car at 2K.

If the user meant something else by "Hybrid" for A, they'll say; the reply on 16 October stated this reading. The
options, as set out to them: both are the same
architecture up to the last layer. The Director, packs from disk, the schema, the codec check and the dashboard over
the video are shared, and they are most of the work. The options differ only in where the car is drawn:
- **Baked first.** `composite.sh` bakes plate, car, wheel, dials, mirrors, glass and screens into each clip offline, and
  the Director plays those clips. This is milestone 1 of the real renderer, not a fork: milestone 2 swaps baked clips
  for plates plus the live compositor, and the Director doesn't change. A baked test is real for:
  - footage playing in a VS Code webview (codec, two video elements, decode cost);
  - the Director's logic (stops when an agent waits, refuel, scenes, dissolves);
  - how the drive feels while coding, and how the dashboard reads over video;
  - the radio in VS Code.

  It does not test:
  - the live compositor's GPU and battery cost in the webview;
  - picking a car;
  - the radio face showing the real station (a baked clip shows the stand-in from `headunit.py`; an HTML overlay on
    `screens.console` would fix that).

  A baked pack is also one car only, against the standard's rule that packs ship plates only.
- **Live from the start.** Build the standard's renderer in full first (port `composite.sh`'s graph to WebGL). That
  means more engine work before any IDE test.
- **Either way**, some of what the standard calls "at play time" is too heavy to run live in a webview: the mirror
  feed's traffic tracking and painting out (`traffic.py`, `mirrorfeed.py`), the spray (`spray.py`) and the sign fix.
  Those get computed when the pack is built and shipped with each clip (a mirror plate, tracks), in both options.
- **Suggested fit with the user's plan (chosen):** step 2 tests milestone 1 (baked). The live compositor is built during step 3,
  and step 4's retest uses it, so the release is tested on the real pipeline. Step 6's hi-res is then plates at 1080p
  and the car at 2K, with nothing to bake.
- This is app work: give it its own branch and PR (e.g. `feat/video-renderer`), apart from the footage branch.
- Milestone 1's dashboard is the hybrid photo car (decision B, below). Use the radio spec at the end of this file, the
  red zones of round 2, and the guiding rule above: in Tour mode, the clean car.

## Radio on the photo car: spec for the video renderer

The user chose this on 14 October. Today's radio is the Radio tab of the console panel at the bottom centre of the 3D
dashboard (`src/webview/ui/radioDeck.ts`). When a video pack plays, the dashboard is a photo of a car (`cars/<id>`), and
that bottom-centre spot is the steering wheel. Each car gives the radio a screen instead.

- **Where.** `car.json` `screens.console` is the screen's glass, `[x, y, w, h]` in the car still's pixels (1200×896; the
  view shows rows 0–675, scaled to the panel's width). Grand tourer: `[902, 472, 164, 154]`, about 175×165 px at 1280×720.
  The face sits under the wheel-and-hands layer: the right hand passes in front of the screen's left edge on turns.
- **Two sizes.**
  - The **face** is always on while the car is shown. It has the deck's head only: FM|CB, LIVE, equalizer lights,
    station, song (artist and title on two lines), power, previous, play/pause, next, and presets 1–6. No SET, volume,
    tone controls or lists. Text sizes: station about 15/190 of the screen's width, song 9.5/190, band and LIVE 8/190.
    `tools/footage/headunit.py` is the stand-in (see fx-19).
  - The **full radio** is the whole deck, in a panel that slides up from the bottom over the lower dashboard, right of
    centre, with the road still in view (fx-18 mockup C). A click on the face (not on a transport or preset button) or
    R opens it; R, Esc or a click outside closes it. It can keep the console's tabs, which makes the screen the
    console's home in the photo car.
- **Lights.** The equalizer lights run whenever the face is on screen, not only on the Radio tab (`Cockpit.syncRadio`
  today). Reduced motion still stills them.
- **CB on the face.** The co-pilot's name and status, and a dot per crew agent, amber while one waits or needs help.
- **Night.** Screens dim to 0.55 + 0.45 × the cabin light (about 70% at night).
- **Cluster display.** `screens.cluster` (grand tourer `[562, 458, 78, 68]`) shows the station and band, and the water
  temperature as a bar from C to H that warms to half over the first 45 s. The temperature is the car's, not the radio's.
