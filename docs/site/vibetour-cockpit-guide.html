Design

The rules we designed by

People pay to see the scenery. Every choice in the cockpit follows from that. Keep these in mind before you move anything.

The view is the product
Nothing sits on the windshield while the car drives: no captions, no strip, no place names. The dials and tabs are there when someone looks for them.
Instruments sit in front of the driver
Gauges live in the cluster behind the wheel and on the dash, where a glance down finds them. Alerts appear on the windshield, in the driver's line of sight.
Speak up only when it matters
Interrupt when input is needed (the car stops, the stop pill says why, the bell lights) or when a phase ends (your turn, arrived, refuel). Nothing else asks for attention.
Say each thing once
One stop pill, not a pill and a card. No warning light that repeats a dial. No windshield line that repeats the Nav screen.
Keep it physically plausible
The speedometer reads 0 when the car is stopped. On the photo car, the car's own dials show the car. The car stops where a stop really is, and every name agrees: signs, Nav, stop pill.
Colour means one thing
Red: the car stopped for you. Orange: something needs you. Blue: worth a look. A red zone appears only where "a lot" is bad (Issues, Changes, low Context, many agents at once), never on Tools.
Text stays narrow
Messages wrap inside a pill at most 440 px wide and grow taller. Nobody reads a sentence stretched across the windshield.
Every screen decides for itself
The VS Code panel and a Companion Display keep their own settings. The screen reader hears everything the eye sees.

One big centre screen?

You can build it: move the dials into the centre screen and drop the side clusters. VibeTour's cockpit is deliberately the opposite. A screen off to the side pulls the driver's eyes away from the road on every glance, and that is the distraction this project exists to avoid. If you build one anyway, keep the stop pill and the bell on the windshield, in front of the driver.

Before you share

Keep it working

  • npm run check: types, unit tests and a build.
  • npm run test:e2e: the browser tests, the demo driven like a person would.
  • npm run cockpit-map: re-takes the Cockpit Map's pictures after a visual change. Its key lives in tools/cockpit-map/states.mjs.
  • Update docs/signal-map.md when the wiring changes. It's documentation, not configuration: editing it changes nothing in the app.
  • Check a phone width and Tour mode. A change that looks right on the dash can crowd the windshield.

The Cockpit Map shows every control on every screen. The Signal Map has the wiring behind them. Source: github.com/davidsparrow/vibetour.
